Anthony “Tony” Aiello

Anthony Aiello, known in Wisconsin nightlife as DJ Tonya, began DJing in Racine in 1980 and spent two decades at La Cage in Milwaukee, where he became the house DJ. He reported dance-music charts for the international music magazine Billboard for five years. His work also included PrideFest programming and audio engineering. He first studied computer science around the time he began DJing and returned to finish his degree three decades later. He went on to work in software development, enterprise IT, and computer science education.

Racine and the first years as a DJ

Aiello was born in 1962 and grew up on Racine’s north side in an Italian and Polish American family. His father immigrated from Italy as a teenager. Aiello attended Gilmore Junior High School and Horlick High School. In a 2026 Wisconsin LGBTQ History Project interview with Michail Takach, he recalled that a friend introduced him to a discussion group at a Unitarian Universalist church during his senior year. Through people he met there, he learned about Jo’Dee’s International, a Racine gay bar run by Judy Kunza.

Aiello began working weekends at Jo’Dee’s in 1980. He shared the booth with Richard Doss, who lent him records while he built his own collection. During this period, Aiello also began studying computer science at UW–Parkside. He dates his first enrollment to about 1979 or 1980. In his account, roommates forced him out of his housing after learning he was gay, took his car without permission, and abandoned it. He moved back in with his parents and withdrew from school. The account of the housing incident comes from Aiello’s recollection.

La Cage, 1984–2004

Aiello submitted a homemade cassette mix when La Cage was preparing to open in Milwaukee’s Walker’s Point neighborhood. He worked the private soft opening in March 1984 and initially alternated with head DJ Ty King, playing R&B and hip-hop alongside King’s Top 40 sets. After King left, Aiello became the house DJ. He recalled that his stage name came from running together Tony A. He also built indexes and databases to find records quickly when patrons called the booth with requests.

Aiello’s November 1997 column “A Decade Of Dancing” in The Wisconsin Light provides a contemporary account of his work. Writing under the DJ Tonya byline, he traced changes in the music played at La Cage and its Dance Dance Dance room, which opened in 1987. The column names the songs and artists he associated with different years on the dance floor. A surviving segment of Dance Gay America, recorded around 1996, documents the room itself. Aiello recalled supplying the production crew with a direct audio feed from the booth.

Record pools, Billboard, and PrideFest

Aiello joined Chicago’s Independent Record Service (IRS) Record Pool in 1990. He traveled to Chicago with DJ Kimberly Anne for record-pool releases and imports, including Italian house, Euro dance, and hi-NRG records. Aiello dates the start of his reporting to 1990 or 1991 and confirms he was on Billboard magazine’s Club Chart Panel by 1992. After joining the panel as a reporter for the club dance charts, he received promotional records and other materials directly from record labels. The Wisconsin LGBTQ History Project reports that he served for five years. A separate contemporary record, a 2002 issue of Quest, prints a La Cage Top 15 under his name. Nicole Brand’s “Forever” topped that list.

Aiello says records identify him as part of YaG Productions, a Milwaukee company owned by Dennis Deida. On May 13, 1994, the team booked CeCe Peniston at Club Nitro in Milwaukee. Aiello identifies it as their first major artist booking at a local Milwaukee club under the YaG Productions name.

Aiello recalls starting the first Battle of DJs at MAPFest, a Milwaukee AIDS Project fundraiser, and producing and programming the Dance Pavilion during the first four Milwaukee PrideFests. He described the pavilion as a place for DJs from local LGBTQ clubs to play for a festival audience. The print record also documents appearances outside his regular La Cage sets. A 2000 Quest advertisement billed him at the afterparty for Madison’s M.A.G.I.C. Picnic, and the 2014 Milwaukee PrideFest program lists him on the Rainbow Stage.

Audio engineering and a return to computing

Aiello left his regular La Cage role in 2004, during a period of serious health problems that he discussed publicly in the History Project interview. He joined Custom Designed Lighting, Sound & Video as an audio engineer and became general manager within a year, according to that account. The company designed and installed sound, lighting, and related systems for Milwaukee venues. Aiello also built an early e-commerce storefront for the business, drawing on his familiarity with the equipment it sold.

Aiello says he began planning his return to college around 2010. The History Project dates his formal reenrollment at UW–Parkside to 2011. He returned to mathematics coursework before moving through the computer science curriculum. In May 2018, he earned a BS in Computer Science, with a minor in web development and certificates in cybersecurity and Unix system administration, according to his App Factory profile. He went on to complete an MS in Computer and Information Systems. His current portfolio identifies his concentration as cybersecurity and secure software development. The older App Factory profile, written while he was a graduate student, describes it as software development.

Software, enterprise IT, and community projects

Aiello joined the App Factory in 2017. His archived profile credits him on the On the Radar and Transit App teams and lists website work for the App Factory and the Consortium for Computing Sciences in Colleges’ Midwest conference. In On the Radar, the team used MongoDB, Express, Vue, and Node.js to build a planning prototype. Its radar chart showed which requirements a set of course choices would cover. Aiello’s individual work included Project Volga, an online bookstore for which he built the storefront and connected the catalog to the back end. During graduate study, he also worked on TiX-SOCIAL, a prototype that explored facial recognition for event admission.

In the career history Aiello has shared directly, he reports working in enterprise IT at Innovative Dynamic Networks in 2018–2019 and again in 2021–2022. He has also described running Still Matter, a 3D-printing business with a WooCommerce storefront. His community web work includes Mission Got Your Six, a project for a veteran-led organization serving veterans and first responders. Aiello volunteered in 2018, former classmates helped build the first site, and it launched in June 2019. He says he continued to contribute hosting, the domain, and maintenance after launch.

University work and research

Aiello began teaching Linux courses as an adjunct while completing graduate study and became a full-time computer science lecturer at UW–Parkside in 2022. His account of his work includes introductory programming, Linux and systems administration, database management, cloud computing, and systems analysis and design. He reports taking on interim management and advising for the MS CIS program beginning in summer 2024. UW–Parkside also lists him as an advisor to the Computer Club.

In 2023, UW–Parkside named Aiello a co-principal investigator on its part of a multi-institution National Science Foundation project studying active learning and participation in computing. His publication record includes two coauthored IEEE conference papers on AI-enhanced learning assistants. The 2024 Frontiers in Education paper described the AIELA program. The 2025 SoutheastCon paper compared two implementations of the prototype and addressed issues of scalability, privacy, and accessibility.

Return to La Cage

On March 27, 2026, Aiello returned to DJ La Cage’s 42nd anniversary, 22 years after leaving his regular position there. The return brought him back to the venue whose opening he had worked in 1984. In reflecting on the anniversary, he said the early Dance Pavilion had given local club DJs a chance to reach a larger audience. His 1997 column, the surviving club footage, the published dance chart, and the PrideFest program record different parts of that career while it was happening.
